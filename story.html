Our Story

How it started

The men’s club started in 2022. A few lads already playing pickup in Rathmines wanted a proper league, so they ran a trial and put it out on Reddit, Twitter and Instagram expecting a handful of people. Eighty came.

One team was never going to cover that, so we entered two, fifteen players each, both in Division 7, home games at St Mary’s.

Four seasons on, one of those squads has gone up three years running and plays in Division 4. The other went up for the first time last season and plays in Division 6. Most of the original two squads are still here, including the two who started it. The ones who have stopped playing turn up for a game or a pint at least once a season.

A season

October to the end of March. A division is eight to ten teams, so that is sixteen to eighteen games, half at home, most on a Friday night. Home games are at St Mary’s on Lower Rathmines Road. No entry fee, anyone can walk in. Away nights take us around Dublin and out as far as Naas and Balbriggan.

Pre-season is September, three or four weeks, twice a week. Fitness first, then offence and defence, then getting everyone playing the same system again before the first game.

Once we are going, both squads train Monday nights at the Swan Centre, and Fridays when there is no game. There is also an open scrim at the Swan on Thursdays, pay as you go, anyone can play, and it is where a lot of people meet the club for the first time.

On a double header, whichever team is not playing works the table. After a home game we go to the Blackbird, and Hillbilly’s if anyone is still hungry. Around that there are pub quizzes, NBA and college games watched together, an awards night and an AGM.

Rathmines

Rathmines runs like a village inside the city, and that is why the club is here. The founders lived in it, the hall was in it, and there was already a club in it, but the feel of the place is what made it worth building on.

Most of the squad lives in Dublin 6 or beside it, Rathmines, Rathgar, Ranelagh.

Basketball is what brought everyone together. The people are why they stayed. We are still competitive, still training to win, still watching where we finish in the table. Around that it has turned into a group of mates. People who met at a trial four years ago now work together, help each other out when something needs doing, and see each other nowhere near a basketball court. Friendships came out of this club that would not have existed otherwise.

Getting involved

The squads are full for this season. New players come in between seasons at trials, and a trial is open to anyone. Most people who turn up live nearby or know someone at the club, but a message out of the blue works just as well.

Once the league is running the squads do not change and training is not open to drop-ins, though you are welcome to come and watch. The Thursday scrim at the Swan runs all year and anyone can play in that.

Instagram is the quickest way to reach us, @rathmines_bc for the men and @rathminesballerz for the ladies, and the email addresses are on the contact page. If you would sooner help than play, the table needs someone at every home game and takes no qualification. Refereeing takes a course, and the club will pay for it if you are willing to do it.

Home games are free, so the easiest way in is to turn up on a Friday.
