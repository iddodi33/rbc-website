Our Story

How it started

A few people and a hall

The men’s club started in 2022. A few lads who were already playing pickup in Rathmines wanted to play in a proper league, so they ran a trial and put it out on Reddit, Twitter and Instagram, expecting a handful of people. Eighty came. One team was not going to cover that, so the club entered two instead, fifteen players each, both in Division 7, with home games at St Mary’s.

Four seasons on, one of those two has been promoted three years running and plays in Division 4. The other went up for the first time last season and plays in Division 6. The ladies’ team was here before either of them. Phil and Josh, who started the whole thing, still play for the D6 side, and most of the original two squads are still around. The ones who have stopped playing turn up for a game or a pint at least once a season.

The year

What a season looks like

The league runs from October to the end of March. A division is eight to ten teams, so a season is sixteen to eighteen games, half of them at home, most on a Friday night. Home games are at St Mary’s on Lower Rathmines Road, there is no entry fee, and anyone can walk in and watch. Away nights take us around Dublin and out as far as Naas and Balbriggan. The top two in a division are promoted on league position. Separately, the top four go into a playoff in late April.

Pre-season starts in September, three or four weeks of it, twice a week. Fitness to start, then the offence and the defence, and getting everyone playing the same system again before the first fixture. Once the season is on, the two men’s squads train together on Monday nights at the Swan Centre, and again on Friday when there is no game. There is an open scrim at the Swan on Thursdays as well, paid but open to anyone. When one team is playing, the other works the table. After a home game we go to the Blackbird, and Hillbilly’s if anyone is still hungry. Around that there are pub quizzes, NBA and college games watched together, an awards night at the end of the season and an AGM.

Where we are

The neighbourhood

Rathmines runs like a village inside the city, and that is the reason the club is here. The founders lived in it, the hall was in it, and there was already a club in it, but the feel of the place is what made it worth building on. Most of the squad lives in Dublin 6 or beside it, in Rathmines, Rathgar and Ranelagh. A few come across from the north side, from Rialto and the Liberties.

Basketball is what brought everyone together. The people are why they stayed. The club is still competitive, still training to win and still minding where it finishes in the table. Around that it has turned into a group of friends: people who met at a trial four years ago now work together, help each other out when something needs doing, and see each other well away from the hall. Friendships came out of this club that would not have existed otherwise.

Taking part

Getting involved

The squads are full for this season. New players come in between seasons, at trials, and a trial is open to anyone who wants to come to it. Most people who turn up live nearby or know someone at the club, but a message out of the blue works just as well and nobody gets turned away. Once the league is running, squads are not added to, and training is not open to drop-ins, though you are welcome to come and watch.

Instagram is the quickest way to reach us, and the same route works for the ladies’ team. If you would sooner help than play, the table needs someone at every home game and takes no qualification, refereeing does take one, and there is usually something else that needs doing. Home games at St Mary’s are free, so the simplest way in is to turn up on a Friday.
